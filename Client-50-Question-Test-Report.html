02 — The issue you flagged, confirmed

Yes — this is real, and here's exactly why it happens

You described getting an answer credited to a ticket, but the ticket itself had no real answer in it — just a name. That is confirmed, and it's a real, reproducible bug, not something imagined. It happens specifically when someone asks a "how do I fix this?" style question about a topic where the only matching ticket is very thin (a real ticket in this system can be as bare as just its subject line, with no description or resolution notes at all).

Root cause

When the AI can't find a real resolution in the matching ticket, it should say "I don't know" — and it sometimes does exactly that correctly. But in 10 of 17 ticket-related tests, it instead invented plausible-sounding generic troubleshooting steps that are not actually written anywhere in that ticket, and then labeled the thin ticket as the "source" — even though the real content it was drawing from (when it drew from anything real at all) was often a completely different document that never got named.

The good news: this is isolated to the ticket area specifically. Live website search was 16/16 correct, and the knowledge library was 16/17 correct. It's a fixable, well-understood pattern — not a system-wide reliability problem.

07 — Root cause & recommendation

What's actually broken, and the fix

The assistant already has a rule built in that's supposed to say "I don't know" rather than invent an answer when a ticket has no real resolution — and it followed that rule correctly in some cases (#31, #32). But in 10 of 17 ticket tests, it didn't: it generated plausible, generic-sounding troubleshooting steps and attributed them to a ticket that never contained them, or under-reported which document it actually drew the real content from.

Recommended fix: tighten the citation check so an answer can only be shown with a ticket as its source if the specific ticket's own content — checked directly, not assumed — actually contains the claim being made. This is the same class of safeguard already used for follow-up questions (each one is independently double-checked before being shown); the same idea should be applied to the main answer whenever a thin ticket is involved.

This does not affect the live website search area (16/16 correct) or most of the knowledge library (16/17 correct) — it's a targeted fix to one specific path.
